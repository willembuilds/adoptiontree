01The premise

A working use case is a beginning.
Build the ability to do it again.

01 / 06The thread

One working AI use case is not the goal. The ability to repeat it is. The Adoption Tree® Model takes an organization from its first use case to many, and turns that into a transformed way of working.

AI adoption is the ability to turn AI into trusted, governed, used and measurable ways of working. The Adoption Tree® Model connects a successful use case to the capability needed to sustain and repeat it.

01

The data, controls, patterns, skills and ownership that every next wave inherits.

    03The adoption wave

    One domain. Six gates.
    Evidence at every step.

    03 / 06The thread

    Six gates are six steps from use case to adoption: a fixed route so nothing is skipped, a team so everything is recorded, and a named role for everyone. Each wave leaves the organization with learnings it keeps.

    Each gate is a decision with a named owner and minimum evidence. A wave that cannot show the evidence waits, changes or stops. Select a block to see what happens in it, who decides and what it gives you.

    04The capability that stays

    The office is temporary.
    Internal ownership is the outcome.

    04 / 06The thread

    AI keeps moving faster. The capability built here moves with it, because it is owned inside the organization and stays after the Adoption Office has handed over.

    A jointly staffed office runs one domain wave. Internal successors are named on day one and take over the rhythm as the wave goes on; an internal Adoption Capability of trained coaches stays.

    Authority stays with your named roles throughout. What moves is how much outside support each decision needs.

    Step 1 · At Gate 0

    The handover gets a name and a date on day one.

    Gate 0 names the internal successor and writes the mandate’s end date into the charter. The successor shadows the office lead. From Gate 1, an internal Adoption Coach works next to the external one, and the internal share rises.

    Who decides: your named roles, at every gate. The weekly rhythm: the office carries it while your successor shadows. Making, challenging, substituting and exiting are still supported by the office.

    Why it matters to you

    The exit is designed before the work starts. Successors know the date by which they must be ready, and the sponsor gets a scheduled decision.

    Step 2 · From Gate 3

    Your owners take the chair while support is still in the room.

    From Gate 3, internal owners chair the meetings, sign and present. Decisions are prepared jointly with the office, and external support reduces as the wave goes on.

    Who decides: your named roles, at every gate. The weekly rhythm: your owners chair it and the office supports. Making, challenging, substituting and exiting are prepared jointly.

    Why it matters to you

    Your people take real decisions with support at hand, before they have to take them alone.

    Step 3 · Before Gate 5

    Leaving is measured, not promised.

    Your teams now run the routines and chair the decisions while the office stands aside. These are the main transfer conditions, each evidenced rather than asserted:

    1. Internal teams complete two consecutive four-week review cycles without the office, including one without the lead coach.
    2. A named internal owner, not the Adoption Coach, produces and signs a real release pack without external drafting, and a control owner accepts it.
    3. The same owners chair the next wave’s mandate and selection decisions while the external team observes in silence.
    4. The value owner has stated the twelve-month run cost and the domain owner has accepted it into a named budget line.
    5. Licenses, contracts, audit rights and exit dependencies are explicit, with no lock-in on the office’s tooling.
    6. Transfer does not depend on one person: there are two trained coaches per domain, or a coach and a trained deputy.

    A third party can check the exit test from the paperwork alone. The transfer scorecard is a self-assessment by the office being tested. When a wave is high-risk, or further work is under discussion, verification should pass to a party with no stake, such as internal audit.

    Who decides: your named roles, at every gate. The weekly rhythm: your people run it and the office observes in silence. Making, challenging, substituting and exiting are now exercised independently.

    Why it matters to you

    A wave that cannot show this evidence waits, whatever the calendar says.

    Step 4 · Gate 5 and day 90

    The office leaves, and the capability stays with your people.

    At Gate 5 the external team’s part ends: the dossier is signed, the assets are owned internally and the office leaves. Authority stayed with your named roles throughout, and the Adoption Coach stays. Ninety days later, a light review checks the five things the first months erode:

    1. Controls still operate and evidence is filed.
    2. Incidents and support went through the internal route.
    3. Adoption held or improved.
    4. One model or vendor change was absorbed without the office, or, where none occurred, a change drill was documented.
    5. The reuse register and its assets were maintained.

    Who decides: your named roles. The weekly rhythm: your people own it and the coach stays. The office has left.

    Why it matters to you

    The capability lives inside your organization. The next wave starts on the assets this one left behind.

    Measure what changed.

    08A few useful distinctions

    Before you start.

    Who is the model for?

    Enterprise sponsors, domain leaders, product and delivery teams, architects, control owners and workforce leaders who need AI to become a dependable way of working. The whitepaper is oriented toward the European enterprise context.

    Is this a technology platform or a consulting service?

    It is an operating model: a way to connect business ownership, production delivery, responsible operation and internal capability. Its tools can be used alongside your existing platforms and governance.

    Does every wave need to build a new foundation?

    No. Each wave should inherit approved assets and leave useful additions behind. Readiness is assessed before Gate 0. If it is too weak, the first step is a readiness project or a smaller use case.

    Does temporary mean every external specialist must leave?

    No. A chosen, priced and replaceable specialist can remain. What should decline is dependence on a particular external party to make decisions, challenge them, substitute a supplier or exit an arrangement.

    Who pays for it after the office leaves?

    A named budget line does, agreed before the handover rather than at it. The cost side is built at the same gates as the benefit side: a candidate envelope at Gate 0, a first run-cost estimate at Gate 2, metered consumption during bounded production, and both halves measured at Gate 4. At Gate 5 the domain owner accepts the run cost into a budget line.

    Has the model been validated in practice?

    Version 1.1 is published as a design proposal. It combines established ideas; the complete configuration has not yet been evaluated in practice. The reference case is illustrative and the timings are planning assumptions.
